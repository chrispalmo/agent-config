The completed work supports simplifying the default guidance and calling its purpose **Work continuity**. Preserve consequential information and reliable coordination, while allowing the agent to choose the representation and working method. This is an evidence-informed design recommendation. It is not proof that the proposed replacement outperforms the existing package.

## Basis for the decision

The intended outcome is correct continuation by a capable successor without the originating chat. Judge alternatives by retained intent and constraints, correct understanding of completed and unfinished work, avoided duplication, human corrections, and the total effort of maintaining and recovering context. Document length and number of files are secondary. Human readability and cross-harness portability are deliberate user needs, not deficiencies to optimize away.

The evidence separates **information that must survive** from **instructions about how an agent should work**. An agent may outgrow a procedural workaround. It cannot infer an arbitrary unrecorded human decision with confidence. Durable state can nevertheless be stale, misleading or ignored, so merely writing a note does not establish successful continuity.

## Where the completed assessments agree

The prior-knowledge responses converge closely on a proportionate outcome requirement. Start with existing artifacts, preserve otherwise unrecoverable intent and the non-obvious reasons behind consequential decisions, keep state current, and make it discoverable. Neither recommends imposing a complete project lifecycle, fixed document tree or permanent hierarchy of agent roles.

The two completed literature reviews support that direction while qualifying it. Preserving predecessor information can reduce rediscovery, but a particular summary format is not uniformly best. Useful domain expertise can help, while generic guidance can add cost or fail to help. Current models can still violate a recorded constraint. These are reasons to keep the continuity obligation and reconsider the procedural package around it.

This agreement has limited evidential weight of its own. The prior-knowledge pair shared a skill; the literature reviews share some sources; and the lead already knew the discussion. The strength of the recommendation comes primarily from the fit between the user's goal and the verified evidence, not a vote count among models.

## Where the differences matter

**Capability growth does not imply monotonically less structure.** Fable expects the necessary record to shrink as agents reconstruct more from artifacts. Astra explicitly allows improved capability to make richer indexing or retained evidence worthwhile. The useful conclusion is to reduce unnecessary prescription, not to adopt a permanent word limit or assume that every future system should be smaller.

**A lightweight default still needs an answer to discovery and freshness.** Fable proposes a conventional entry point and consequence-based update triggers; Astra allows existing trackers, notebooks and other durable systems. Neither establishes a universal filename. Reuse the existing authoritative location and add a pointer only when discovery otherwise fails.

**Human authority is an invariant; a human-maintained section is an implementation choice.** Fable suggests a human-controlled constraints section. Astra emphasizes provenance and not widening authorization. Preserve the latter requirement without automatically adding a manual editing gate: agents can accurately record explicit human decisions while keeping their own interpretations distinguishable.

**Some procedures earn their place.** The literature review is less dismissive of targeted skills than a broad “agents have outgrown guidance” claim. Removing generic workflow ceremony should not remove genuinely missing domain knowledge or a procedure that prevents a demonstrated consequential failure.

## Evidence that changes the decision

The [source tables](results/lead-literature.md#evidence-table) and [verification record](verification.md) retain dates, comparators and limitations. Five findings are particularly consequential:

1. [Handoff Debt](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02875v2) studies actual coding continuation and supports preserving predecessor context to reduce rediscovery. It does not identify a universally winning schema or include all preparation costs.
2. [HSC-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36523v1), published 27 October, gives newer handoff evidence, but results depend on the receiver and scoring definition. It does not establish our exact contract's superiority.
3. [Evaluating AGENTS.md](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v3) and [TimelineBench](https://timelinebench.tensortest.com/docs/agents#curated-editorial-guidance) caution against assuming that general instructions improve outcomes. [SkillsBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670v4) supplies positive counterevidence for curated expertise. These interventions should not be conflated.
4. [MemoryTax](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29823v1) supports giving capable agents access to useful original evidence instead of assuming lossy advance extraction is best. Its raw-history baseline does not remove the need to preserve information outside an unavailable originating harness.
5. [Beyond Gain](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32322v1), published 23 October, clarifies retirement: equal poor performance with and without guidance does not show the model has mastered the task. An ineffective instruction can be removed while the underlying failure remains unresolved.

Recent public discussion reinforces concern about scope, authority, corrections and recoverability, but does not establish a community consensus or causal improvement. The two completed literature reviews found no direct evaluation of this repository's current package against its proposed replacement.

## Recommended maintenance decision

**Retain simplification as a proposal for when the icebox conditions are met.** Keep the previously accepted short contract as the starting point. Use this research to prevent accidental loss of important information, not to turn the replacement into another large framework.

Preserve the requirement to make consequential intent, constraints, decisions and reasons, evidence, and unfinished work discoverable. Preserve reconciliation when contributors overlap. Make verification status, uncertainty and superseded decisions clear when consequential. These are information needs, not mandatory headings or separate files.

Remove or demote assumptions about building software, project phases, task tables, a mandatory next-action field, and repeated procedural checklists where those assumptions do not serve the work. Retain examples or templates as optional aids for demonstrated gaps. Existing project-specific verification, permissions and user preferences remain authoritative.

Do not require Markdown copies of facts already governed by a tracker or artifact. Markdown remains a useful portable default where no existing authoritative record works. A portable evidence archive may help, but it should not force every successor to recover the whole original conversation before it can act.

For multiple agents, keep ownership and update reconciliation proportional to actual overlap. A prose rule does not itself make simultaneous edits safe; existing version control, a shared tracker or an appropriate update mechanism may still be necessary. Add mechanisms for observed contention rather than treating every task as a coordination-system design problem.

Use observed resumption failures and maintenance friction to refine the result. If the user declines a formal evaluation, that is sufficient for a reversible local design decision. It is not sufficient to claim a quantified reliability or productivity improvement. No new benchmark is proposed as a prerequisite here.

## Naming

**Work continuity** best meets the naming criteria: it covers coding, research, writing and operations; names the desired outcome; and does not imply project planning, scheduling or governance. Both independent Astra reports arrived at this name without seeing the earlier suggestion. That convergence is reassuring but is not experimental evidence about names.

“Handoff note” works for one artifact or transfer. “Recoverable state” describes a property. “Project management” describes a broader responsibility than the proposed small guidance supplies. Rename only if resumed maintenance actually changes the scope; no rename is implemented by this review.
