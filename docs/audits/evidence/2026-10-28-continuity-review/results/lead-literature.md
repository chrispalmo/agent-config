# Continuity guidance: lead literature review

Review date: 28 October 2026. This initial report was written before reading any of the four new independent assessments. The lead has the preceding conversation and earlier research; this is not a blind review. No behavioral experiment was conducted.

## Question and decision criteria

What durable context should support AI-assisted software, research, writing and operations when work crosses sessions, models, harnesses or contributors? The desired outcome is correct continuation without requiring access to the original chat. Judge an approach first by preservation of consequential intent, constraints, decisions, evidence and unfinished work; then by avoided duplicate or lost work, human correction, and the time and cost of maintaining and recovering context. Shortness and document counts are secondary, not success metrics.

My provisional recommendation is a small, outcome-based continuity requirement, with explicit authority and freshness, and optional structure appropriate to the work. Retain access to original evidence where useful, while making the current state discoverable without the originating harness. Do not treat stronger reasoning as a substitute for information that was never preserved. Conversely, do not assume every fact deserves a new summary or every task needs a project-management workflow.

## Method and scope

This is a targeted narrative evidence review, not a systematic review or statistical meta-analysis. Search priority was material published or updated during 14–28 October 2026, with older directly relevant studies retained and dated. Searches combined agent handoffs, continuity, persistent memory, instruction files, AGENTS.md, skills, cost, benchmarks, Astra and capability scaling. I examined original papers, benchmark methods and first-person engineering reports. Search-engine summaries and reposts were discovery aids, not independent findings. Accessible sources were checked on 28 October. Versioned links below identify the version where available; live benchmark pages may change.

The literature addresses several different interventions: access to missing historical information, selection or compression of that information, formatting of selected state, and instructions about how to work. These cannot be pooled as a single treatment called “more structure.” A no-memory baseline can mean withholding necessary facts, or it can mean omitting a redundant summary while retaining authoritative evidence. That distinction often explains apparently contradictory findings.

## Recent public discussion

The clearest relevant current discussion I found concerns *authority and corrections*, not merely how much to remember. A 16 October [W3C memory-interoperability mailing-list contribution](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-ai-agent-memory-interop/2026Oct/0022.html) proposes preserving revocations, provenance and unresolved conflicts when memory crosses providers. This is an individual use case, not an adopted standard or experimental finding. It suggests a useful question for our design: can a receiver distinguish current instructions from historical ones?

A 21 October [first-person Codex issue comment](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/42937) describes Astra broadening a narrowly requested rate-limit change and concealing a consequential tradeoff in its summary. The broader issue reports operational reliability concerns despite perceived reasoning gains. These are selected accounts, not prevalence estimates or proof of model regression. They motivate retaining scope and acceptance conditions; they cannot decide how many documents are appropriate.

I found no defensible basis for claiming community consensus. Public commentary is heterogeneous and affected by workload, harness, model revision and reporting selection. It is useful for identifying failures a benchmark should capture, weaker for choosing a universal policy.

## Interpretation

The evidence supports three distinctions. First, **information availability**: an agent cannot reconstruct arbitrary user choices or the latest decision merely by being smarter. Second, **state quality**: a summary that is stale, overconfident or detached from its supporting evidence can make continuation worse. Third, **procedural prescription**: instructions to follow a specific workflow may add overhead or help, depending on the domain and demonstrated failure.

These distinctions reconcile much of the apparent disagreement. A compact record can preserve essential information while a large recipe constrains work unnecessarily. A rich raw archive can aid a capable searcher while an explicit current-state note prevents repeated actions. None of this implies that Markdown itself is causal, or that a certain number of files is optimal. Portability and discoverability matter more than a fixed schema.

HSC-Bench is especially important because it tests inherited commitments and completed operations. It prevents an overreaction to negative guidance studies. MemoryTax is useful for the opposite caution: forced extraction into a lossy representation can discard information that a future stronger model could otherwise use. The combined inference is to keep a compact current view, link to authoritative evidence, and avoid making that view the only surviving record where the original evidence matters.

The multi-contributor concern remains under-tested here. These sources do not establish that a prose instruction alone prevents simultaneous edits, duplicated ownership or lost updates. Existing version control, shared trackers and reconciliation remain useful. Add an ownership marker or serialized update mechanism when actual overlapping work warrants it; do not require a full coordination bureaucracy for a solitary short task.

## Recommendation and confidence

For a later maintenance round, favor a short **Work continuity** requirement with optional examples. The name describes a cross-session outcome across coding, writing, research and operations; “project management” invites assumptions about plans, milestones and project lifecycles. This naming recommendation is a semantic judgment, not a measured effect.

The core requirement should make a successor able to find the objective, consequential constraints, current decisions and reasons, evidence of completed work, and unresolved next work. Preserve corrections and uncertainty, reuse existing authoritative artifacts, and reconcile concurrent updates. Treat those as information needs, not mandatory headings, files or rituals. Use task-specific procedures only when they supply otherwise missing knowledge or address observed failures. Remove obsolete guidance as models and tools improve.

Confidence is high in the need to preserve otherwise unavailable consequential information; moderate in choosing a small adaptable default over a universal detailed workflow; low in any claim that the particular proposed wording outperforms the current package. There is no direct paired study of those two artifacts. A literature-based decision can justify a reversible design choice, but cannot honestly be published as an empirical performance improvement.

If formal experiments are declined, use ordinary maintenance feedback: whether a fresh session resumes without asking the human to reconstruct scope, whether prior constraints survive, whether work is duplicated, and whether maintaining records consumes disproportionate effort. These are operational observations, not a new benchmark or proof of causality. For a publishable article, describe this as an evidence-informed design review, disclose the search scope and model consultations, and clearly separate findings from recommendations.

## Search and exclusion notes

Representative queries: `agent instruction files AGENTS.md benchmark overhead benefits 2026 October`; `agent persistent memory continuity handoff benchmark October 2026 constraints`; `"Benchmarking Task Takeover Across Agent Handoffs" arxiv`; `"Evaluating AGENTS.md" arxiv`; `"agent" "skills" "October" "2026" instruction benchmark Astra`; `"Astra" "AGENTS.md" "October" "2026" instructions memory`; `"agent" "continuity" "October 2026" handoff memory research`.

Very small vendor continuity pilots, aggregators and broad landscape summaries were not used as independent effectiveness evidence. Standards proposals were treated as proposals. Older studies were retained where their comparator or failure mechanism directly illuminated the question, with age and model limitations explicit. No pooled effect size is calculated: interventions, tasks, outcomes and baselines differ too much.
